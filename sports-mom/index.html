One setup · one season

The Sports Mom Issue

Your kid’s season, handled. You talk once. It runs until the season ends.

Setup: ~10 minutes Upkeep: 5 min/week Runs: all season

What you do

10 min, one time
  1. 1

    Make the Project

    In Claude, tap Projects, then Create Project. Name it after your kid’s season. “Marcus Fall Soccer.”

  2. 2

    Paste this into the Project’s custom instructions

    Interview me about my kid's sports season, one question at a time. Ask up to five questions covering: the specific thing I'm dreading, the practice and game schedule, any food restrictions or constraints I have to work around, and what I want to stop thinking about once this is set up. Don't write anything until we're done. Then write my Project instructions in my own words: what this tracks, what it flags on its own, and what it should ask me when something changes.

    The interview prompt
  3. 3

    Start a chat in the Project and answer the questions

    Talk the way you’d talk to a friend. Messy is fine. When it asks for the schedule, take a photo of whatever the team handed you and send it.

    When it’s done, it writes your instructions. Copy them. Go back into the Project settings and paste them over the interview prompt.

    That’s it. Setup is done. You never run the interview again.

  4. 4

    Turn on Google Calendar

    In the Project, connect Google Calendar. This is what lets it put the games on your real calendar instead of just telling you about them.

What it handles

  • The schedule. Every practice and game goes on your calendar from the photo you sent. When the coach changes something mid-season, send the new photo. It updates instead of you retyping dates.
  • The group chat. Screenshot it, send it, ask what you need to know. It pulls out the three things that need you and drops the forty that don’t.
  • Snack duty. It knows your date and it knows the allergy or the vegan kid, because you said so in the interview. It flags two days out with a store list that already works around the restriction. You are not re-explaining the dairy thing every three weeks.
  • Practice-night homework. You told it what nights are tight. It knows Tuesday is a 30-minute window between practice and bed, and plans homework help around that instead of pretending you have a normal evening.
  • The skill the coach mentioned. Whatever the coach said your kid needs to work on becomes a short at-home drill you can actually run in a driveway. Not a training program. Five minutes.
  • End of season. Coach gift, team party, the whole thing surfaces before the last game instead of the night before.
  • Emergencies. Uniform stain at 8pm the night before a game. Ask it. It answers for that fabric and that stain.

How it runs

  • Every Sunday
    Five minutes.

    Open the Project. Paste the week’s group chat. Ask what’s coming.

  • As it happens
    Whenever a schedule changes.

    Send the photo.

  • Once a season
    Mid-season, if something big shifts.

    New coach, kid moves up a division, tell it. It updates its own instructions.

You never start a new chat from scratch. It remembers the season.

What it can’t do

No surprises

Straight answers, so nothing surprises you later.

  • ×It can’t buy anything. It’ll build the list and hand it to you ready to order, but you place the order.
  • ×It can’t post in the team group chat. It reads screenshots, it doesn’t join the chat.
  • ×It can send email if you connect Gmail, but only after showing you the draft first.

The one thing that matters

Most apps organize your load and hand it back to you. You end up maintaining the house and the app tracking the house.

This is different because you set it up once and then talk to it like a person who already knows the situation. There is no system to maintain. There is nothing to keep updated. You talk, it handles it.
